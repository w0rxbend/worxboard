`just model order production` (case revision **A5**, which changes the machined parts only, deviation
CASE-10: the printed parts are as in A3 and A4; in A3 the spacers' cut-out round the TRRS jack grew to
0.75 mm round the jack body; A2 gave it R1.5 corners; since A3 the spacers also have the stabiliser
wire channels, deviation ORDER-6); CI fails if they drift from the model. Upload the STL files. STEP versions of the same parts are in `case/exports` after
`just model` and in the `worxboard-case` CI artifact.

## Leg caps: why a tree

The cap is a Ø9 × 8.5 mm push-on boot for the leg's Ø4.5 spigot, and it has to grip the desk, so it
must be flexible. JLC3DP's only flexible material is TPU 95A, which it prints by FDM only, with a
**minimum part size of 30 × 30 × 10 mm** (3D Printing Design Guideline, build-size table; its TPU
page gives 30 × 30 × 15, so the tree meets the larger figure). One cap is far below that. The MJF and SLA materials accept 5 × 5 × 5 mm
parts, but every one of them is rigid (PA12, PA11, resins), and a rigid cap slides on the desk.

So the caps are ordered as one TPU part: 8 caps standing on their open ends on a 1.6 × 1.6 mm runner
frame, with spokes to a Ø8 × 16 mm tag post in the middle that makes the part 16 mm tall
(`build_leg_cap_tree` in `case/worxboard_case.py`; the model checks it is one piece, at least
30 × 30 × 15 mm, with every bore open). Walls: cap 2.35 mm, runners 1.6 mm (JLC3DP TPU: 1.6 standard,
1.2 minimum).

Remarks to paste:

```
TPU 95A, FDM. One part: 8 small caps joined by runners, please print as uploaded (open ends of the caps
down on the bed, domes up; no supports needed). The bores (dia. 4.3 x 5 deep) must stay open.
```

After delivery: cut each cap off the runner flush at its base with side cutters, then push it onto the
leg spigot (Ø4.5, 0.2 mm interference on the Ø4.3 bore). If a cap is loose (FDM tolerance ±0.3 mm),
add a drop of flexible glue.

**Fallback** if JLC3DP rejects the tree or the caps come out poorly: print `worxboard_leg_cap.stl`
(in `case/exports`, or the CI artifact `worxboard-case`) in any TPU 95A yourself, 6 pieces, open end
down; or fit the stock ErgoDox EZ rubber caps, which may fit the Ø4.5 spigot (not checked).

## Plate spacers

**Leave the spacers out of the first order** (deviation CASE-10). The case works without them: the
plate is milled into the top shell and nothing rests on the spacer. Its switch openings are 15.0 mm,
0.5 mm per side round the 14.0 mm lower housing, while the project's own keepout for a switch housing
with its plate clips is 15.6 mm (`layout.keys.HOUSING`). The spacer lies directly under the 1.5 mm
plate, floats 0.5 mm and is printed to about ±0.3 mm, so on one side of a switch the clip relief can
reach zero and the switch may not latch in the plate. Whether it does is not proven either way.
Build the keyboard without spacers first. If you want them afterwards (they damp the plate a little),
print one on any FDM printer and check that every switch still clicks into the plate, then order MJF.
Opening the holes to 15.6 mm in the model is not done: they then merge with the clearance holes
round the T5 boss and the build drops four openings (`spacer_check` fails).

The spacer fills the 3.0 mm between the PCB and the plate around every switch. It is not clamped:
it floats 0.5 mm above the PCB and the switches hold it in place, so a little warp does not matter.
Beside each 2u thumb key it has a 3.5 mm wide channel on both sides of the switch, 6.5–10 mm from the
key centre, running from one stabiliser cut-out to the other: the Cherry-style stabiliser's wire bar
lies there, under the plate (`STAB_WIRE_U`; the model checks the channel is open).
MJF PA12 is the recommended process (tough, slightly damping). The plate is 3.0 mm thick, above the
2.0 mm wall JLC3DP gives for a 200 mm MJF part, and the model removes every web narrower than 1.0 mm
(`SPACER_MIN_WEB`); most webs between the switch openings are 4 mm wide.

Remarks to paste:

```
MJF PA12. Thin flat lattice, 3.0 mm thick: please keep it flat (warp up to 0.5 mm is acceptable).
Left and right spacers are mirror images, one of each.
```

Alternative: SLA resin (8228 or 9600) prints it too, but it is brittle.
