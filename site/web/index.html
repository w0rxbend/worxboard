Cut from solid 6061.

Each half is two machined parts: a top shell with the switch plate milled into it, and a tray underneath. Bead-blasted, anodized, 349 g of aluminium per half. It stays where you put it.

Finish: Black

Six colours to try here. The real one is any anodize your machine shop offers.

A light under every key.

74 RGB LEDs sit under the switches and shine up through them. The firmware runs four effects across the whole board, and both halves stay in step.

Effect

Whole-board effects, not a colour per key. On one cable the brightness is capped at 8 %.

A screen where a key used to be.

A 160 × 68 memory LCD replaces the inner top key of each half, behind a raised aluminium bezel. The left one shows your layer and status. The right one shows Caps, Num and Scroll Lock.

Reflective, so it reads in daylight and draws almost nothing. The screens here show placeholder text.

201 parts. You solder five pins.

The factory fits every part on the board: the RP2040, 16 MB of flash, 37 hot-swap sockets, 37 LEDs. You push in the switches. The only soldering is the five header pins of each display.

Any MX-compatible switch fits, 3-pin or 5-pin. Swap them any time, with no soldering.

Three legs. Your angle.

Each half takes three aluminium legs at the mount positions of the ErgoDox EZ. Swing a leg to set the tilt, then lock it with a thumbscrew. A soft foot cap keeps it from sliding.

Held by friction: a washer under the thumbscrew does the work.

Tech specs

Layout
74 keys, 37 per half, in the original ErgoDox positions with 25° thumb clusters. The inner top key of each half gives way to the display.
Switches
MX-compatible, plate-mount, 3-pin or 5-pin, in hot-swap sockets. Four 2u plate-mount stabilisers on the thumbs. A standard 76-key ErgoDox keycap set covers it.
Lighting
37 SK6812MINI-E RGB LEDs per half, one under each switch, shining up through it. ZMK underglow: solid, breathe, spectrum and swirl, with hue and brightness on the thumb keys. Brightness capped at 8 % on one cable.
Display
Sharp LS011B7DH03 memory LCD, 160 × 68, on a nice!view-class module in each half.
Controller
RP2040 on each board, 16 MB of QSPI flash, 12 MHz crystal. No daughterboard.
Connection
USB-C on both halves; the computer plugs into the left. The halves talk over a 3.5 mm TRRS cable at 115 200 baud, which also powers the right half.
Firmware
ZMK, with ZMK Studio for live remapping. Three layers out of the box. Flash by dragging a UF2 file onto the board.
Board
4 layers, 1.6 mm FR4, ENIG, 182.8 × 158.8 mm per half. 201 parts per half placed by JLCPCB or PCBWay, on both sides.
Case
CNC 6061-T6 top shell and tray, a 3 mm printed spacer between them. 192.4 × 165.6 × 17.6 mm per half, 19.6 mm at the display bezel. Eight M2.5 screws from below.
Tenting
Three mounts per half at the ErgoDox EZ positions. Custom aluminium legs with TPU foot caps, held by M3 thumbscrews.
Licence
Free and open source: code under GPL-3.0-or-later, hardware under CERN-OHL-S-2.0.

Four layers of copper

A circuit board is a sandwich: sheets of copper with glass-epoxy between them. This board has four copper sheets. Pick one to see what is on it.

The layer drawings are not in this build. Run just pcb-layers left, then just site.

Layer to show

Four copper sheets in one board. The two outer sheets carry the signals. The two inner sheets are almost solid: one is ground, one feeds the LEDs. 512 plated holes, called vias, join the sheets.

The top side. The RP2040, the USB-C socket and the other small parts sit here. It holds 2,033 track segments on 95 nets, 3,269 mm in all. The fill between the tracks is spare copper tied to ground. The data line that runs from LED to LED is on this layer.

One sheet of ground, with no track on it. Every part that needs ground drops a via straight down to this sheet, so the return current always has a short way back. The small rings are places where other nets pass through without touching it.

One sheet that feeds the 37 LEDs of this half. Where the outer layers were full, the router let 65 short track segments cross it: 87 mm on 13 nets.

The bottom side, under the keys: 37 hot-swap sockets with their diodes, LEDs and capacitors. It holds 1,316 track segments on 110 nets, 2,761 mm in all. On the two-layer rev B the main ground lived here, and the LED tracks cut it into islands.

The left half, seen from the top side, as KiCad plots it. The gaps in the stack are stretched: the real board is 1.6 mm thick.

Cut through the board

With two layers. Ground is whatever copper is left between the tracks. On rev B the LED tracks cut that copper into islands, and the ground checks failed on the left half.

With four layers. Ground is a whole sheet. Every ground pad has a via beside it that goes straight to the sheet, so no track decides the ground path. Both halves now pass with 0 DRC violations.

How you get one

worxboard is free, so nobody sells it. You order it from the factories yourself, with files that are ready to upload, and put it together. No soldering on the boards, no programming.

  • Top shells, trays and legsCNC 6061-T6, bead blast and anodizecase/production/jlccnc/
  • Spacers and foot caps3D-printed PA12 and TPUcase/production/jlc3dp/
  • Switches, keycaps and hardwareFrom any keyboard shop and a screw suppliercase/production/hardware.csv

Cost. Revision A's boards were estimated at about $307 to $425 for both halves, shipping included, on 2026-11-04. Revision B has four layers and 82 more parts per half and has not been re-estimated: read the price from your quote.

Cables. A USB-C data cable, not a charge-only one. A 3.5 mm TRRS cable with four contacts, wired straight through.

Two rules. Plug the computer into the left half. Never plug or unplug the TRRS cable while either half has power: unplug USB from both halves first.

Build it from source

Every file on this page is generated by scripts: the schematic, the routing, the case, the firmware layout. One layout package feeds them all.

uv, just and Docker
$ git clone https://github.com/w0rxbend/worxboard && cd worxboard
$ just setup              # the Python environment
$ just fw                 # ZMK firmware for every target
$ just pcb-all            # both boards from scratch, about 25 minutes each
$ just pcb-stock left 5   # live part stock for the order
$ just model              # the case: STEP, STL, drawings
$ just fitcheck           # the board inside the case: 0 mm3
$ just site-serve         # this page on http://localhost:8000
